# Internals

Contracts for the three parts whose rules aren't obvious from the outside:
masks, verified accept, and alias tables. Thresholds live with the modules that
enforce them. `docs/OPERATIONS.md` has the commands.

## Coordinate frames

Mixing these up is the most common bug in this codebase.

| Thing                                      | Frame                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Annotations, mask detection                | small frame (the reduced copy a model reads)                   |
| GCPs, affines, results, warps              | full-resolution source pixels                                  |
| The on-disk small image (the review ghost) | upright; `rotation_applied` in the manifest where prep rotated |
| `split_overlaps`, mask heal distances      | EPSG:3857 planar meters                                        |
| Stored masks                               | EPSG:4326                                                      |

The per-sheet `scale` in `sheets/manifest.json` is the only small-to-full
conversion. The affine convention is `autogeoref.affine`'s 2×3 `M` with
`[X, Y] = M @ [1, px, py]`, X/Y in EPSG:3857 meters, pixel y down. Volunteer
crop ground truth is not convertible to a whole sheet.

## Review edits

The UI never mutates results. Edits land in
`work/<volume>/review/p<N>.json` and `--apply` materializes them into
`OK (reviewer-verified)` results.

Edits are affine compositions in world space (`world' = L @ world + t`),
composed onto the recorded affine. GCPs are never re-fit in the UI. On apply
the recorded GCPs travel through the same chain, so `fit_affine` over the moved
GCPs reproduces the composed affine exactly.

`OK (reviewer-verified)` sheets are committed-grade. They vouch in
corroboration and join the back half, but they are pinned out of the seam solve
and excluded from every auto-acceptance statistic in `autogeoref.report`.

Mask validity is decided by one test only: a real `gdalwarp` cutline dry-run
(`warp.gdalwarp_cutline_dryrun`), run on save and again at apply. shapely and
OGR validity don't predict GDAL cutline acceptance and don't count.

## Masks

A mask is the cutline bounding what one sheet contributes to the mosaic.

1. **Detect** (`mask.geometry`): bound the sheet in pixels, project the ring
   through the poly1 GCP affine into EPSG:4326. Default is
   `detect_content_box`, the padded bbox of the printed color. A sheet with no
   color, one whose box would cut its own drawn content, and any page in
   `content_mask_exempt` fall back to `detect_page_bounds`'s page rectangle.
   Declared `content_masks` hulls and reviewer `mask_px` rings beat both.
2. **Split** (`split_overlaps`): each sheet keeps the ground nearest its own
   mask centroid. After it, regular sheets are a zero-overlap partition.
3. **Move** (`mask.move.move_blank_cores`): give a winner's blank ground back
   to the neighbor that drew it.
4. **Heal** (`mask.geometry.heal`): clean, shrink 2 cm, 10 cm, 50 cm, 2 m,
   convex hull. Each rung verified by the real cutline dry-run.
5. **Measure** (`mask.qa`): write `masks-qa.json`.

The `_usable` guard in step 2 is **per neighbor**, checked against the running
cell in page order. A sheet may shed more than half its area cumulatively; only
a single cut taking half of what's left is refused. Guarding on the union of
cuts instead lets one oversized neighbor keep its whole mask while the others
trim against it, restoring large amounts of double coverage. All cuts come from
the original geometry, so page order decides which cut is refused, never where
a cutline falls.

`RawSheetMask.rect` is always the page rectangle whatever the mask style,
because the ink raster is measured in it. A raw or split mask is a single
`Polygon`; the blank-core move can detach islands and punch holes, so
everything downstream accepts `MultiPolygon` and holes. Overlay operations on
split finals are grid-snapped and areal only, since a near-degenerate boundary
fold can pass `is_valid` and still answer `intersection` and `difference`
contradictorily.

**Why the color box.** A whole-page mask carries a wide blank margin,
neighboring masks then overlap on blank paper, and the split degenerates into a
Voronoi tessellation of page centroids with cells cutting mid-block. There is
no printed neatline on these plates at any resolution; what looks like one is
the outermost block frontage line, which stops at every cross street. A
detector for it was built and refused: it truncates drawn content on sheets the
color box leaves whole.

**Sheets that sit out of the split** repeat ground the regular pages own and
paint underneath as fallback coverage. `slugs.DuplicateCoverage` carries both
kinds so no two bake stages classify a sheet differently.

- _Declared overview pages_ (`VolumeConfig.overview_pages`), district-scale
  plates. Declared, never derived, since an overview can carry a plain numeric
  id.
- _Skeleton twins_, the uncolored outline duplicate of a numeric sheet. Derived
  but not from the id alone: `13S` is a skeleton only when page `13` is in the
  same volume, because some volumes print the division letter as part of every
  sheet number (`20 S`). Resolved from the volume's whole page inventory in
  `regions/`, so a rejected sheet can't re-class its twin.

**The blank-core move.** Sanborn draws a feature on one sheet and leaves the
same ground blank on the neighbor that also frames it, and the centroid split
has no notion of what's drawn. A winner cell of `BLANK_CORE_M` scale that is
fully blank on the winner and carries at least `MOVE_INK_FLOOR` of a losing
neighbor's ink moves to that neighbor, but only when the winner actually sheds
it. A moved cell must reach the sheet it moves to, directly or through other
moving cells; without that the pass strands islands inside a neighbor. The
coverage-union invariant is checked numerically, and if the move would leave
solid ground uncovered the whole move reverts. Blankness is pixel-strict,
`MOVE_SAMPLES` samples per cell axis. Reviewer `mask_px` sheets and
duplicate-coverage sheets take no part.

### QA flags

`mask.qa` reads only what the bake wrote. Every per-sheet flag is advisory: it
names a sheet in `masks-qa.json`, the report, and `status`, and never refuses a
bake.

| Flag              | Level  | Fires when                                                                         |
| ----------------- | ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `mirrored`        | sheet  | the placement model reflects the scan                                              |
| `outside_window`  | sheet  | the recorded model sits outside the volume's accept-time scale/rotation window     |
| `hull_collapse`   | sheet  | a `hull` sheet's drawn content is largely covered by no regular sheet's final mask |
| `uncovered_ink`   | sheet  | the same measurement at the same bar for a `content_box` sheet                     |
| `blank_overpaint` | sheet  | the mask's blank core paints opaquely over another sheet's committed content       |
| `coverage_gaps`   | volume | uncovered ground in narrow channels between neighboring masks                      |

- **`mirrored`** is a sign test on the model's 2×2 determinant. Not a mask
  defect, but the damage arrives through the mask: `gdalwarp` fills the
  reflected frame with opaque black. `matching.gates_ok` already rejects a
  reflected model at accept time, so this covers the paths that skip it, namely
  a reviewer placement, a migrated record, a new producer. Read it beside
  `axis_perpendicularity` (`|det| / (sx*sy)`, 1.0 for a square placement): a
  singular linear part also gives an arbitrary sign, and such a sheet is broken
  without being mirrored.
- **`outside_window`** re-checks the accept-time window on the model that is
  finally warped, using `matching.gates_ok`'s own bars re-centered per
  `page_scale_multiples`. It covers a real gap: a rescue is accepted on a
  pinned model but records only correspondences, and every consumer refits them
  unconstrained. A reviewer-adjusted placement can legitimately sit outside the
  window, which is why this never gates.
- **`hull_collapse` / `uncovered_ink`** measure the fraction of a sheet's drawn
  content that _no_ regular sheet's final mask covers. Own-mask ink capture is
  not the signal: a correct one-block hull legitimately excludes marginal
  street-name text the neighbor's mask then covers. Same bar, separate names,
  because the bake's rectangle swap answers a collapsed hull and not a color
  box.
- **`blank_overpaint`** is the inverse failure of that swap. The signal is
  contiguous blank patches at least `BLANK_CORE_M` across in the mask's overlap
  with each sheet it covers. Raw low-ink area doesn't work, since under a
  district-wide fill every sheet's street space reads blank. Applies only where
  a volume declares `content_masks`; elsewhere a page rectangle claiming its
  whole ground is the design. The metric is still recorded.
- **`coverage_gaps`** exists because every per-sheet flag is ink-weighted while
  this defect is blank-weighted, so a volume full of holes can report zero
  sheet flags. Uncovered ground inside the union of committed page rectangles
  lying in a channel narrower than twice `COVERAGE_SLOT_RADIUS_M`, per 1000 m²,
  flagged above 0.5. Counts regular sheets and skeleton twins; declared
  overviews stay out of both sides. Calibrated on non-hull volumes, so on a
  `content_masks` volume the flag is uncalibrated.
- **`blank_over_neighbor_m2`** is measured, never flagged. Final masks can't
  see the defect directly, since after the split any final-vs-final overlap is
  identically zero exactly where it lives, so it is measured on split-contested
  ground. The blank-core move runs first, so on a moved bake this is the
  residue the move couldn't recover, and the tripwire for a regression in it.

**Omitted is not clean.** An unmeasurable metric is omitted rather than
guessed. `coverage` and `volume_flags` go missing wholesale when no sheet has a
page rectangle. `covered_frac` can exceed 1.0 and is reported, never gated on.

**Remedies.** Two are automatic: the bake retries a catastrophic
`hull_collapse` sheet as its page rectangle and keeps the swap only if
`bake.auto_exempt_verdict` passes (recorded under `auto_exempted`), and
`bake.expand_content_boxes` grows each box across narrow inter-sheet slots its
own page serves. A color box's `uncovered_ink` has no automatic remedy and is
not a swap candidate. Everything else is an owner decision: an exemption, a
reviewer `mask_px` ring, hull tightening, or a blank-margin clip.

## Verified accept

Every `REJECTED (rescue revoked…)` record carries a provisional placement: a
translation cluster whose anchors all rode one street. `verified_accept` is how
independent evidence can commit it anyway.

**Acceptance needs at least `MIN_CHANNELS` distinct channels voting yes and no
channel refuting.** Each channel yields at most one vote, so two votes from one
channel never suffice. Only revoked-prefix records are considered, so strict,
rescued, and corroborated counts can't move. Acceptance rises only through the
status this stage writes, e.g. `OK (verified: junction+addresses)`.

The three channels:

- **corroboration**: shared centerline nodes agreeing with committed
  neighbors. It needs one node at full tolerance plus a second within twice the
  measured agreement-noise band (`corroborate.is_corroborated_near`). The
  standalone two-node reinstatement gate runs first, so this channel's yes votes
  come from the near-fringe population that gate leaves revoked. This channel is
  never restricted by the allow-list and always votes, because it needs no
  producer stage, no model spend, and no city-specific data.
- **junction**: the advisory verdict junction-verify recorded. It either
  supports or abstains, and it cannot refute. A legacy baked `"supports": false`
  reads as abstain.
- **addresses**: cross-model consensus house numbers, voting block-level. This
  is **the only channel permitted to refute**, because a printed number landing
  in no block is evidence against a placement rather than absence of evidence.

### The addresses channel

The vote is block-level. Numeral world positions carry tens of meters of noise
from range interpolation and frontage offset, so the vote never compares
against an interpolated point. A numeral is _in-block_ when the implied
position sits on its hinted street (perpendicular distance within
`PERP_TOL_M`) **and** the street's interpolated house number there is within
`addr_tol_numbers` (0.75 × block) of the printed value. Both terms are needed:
a plain distance-to-segment test can't see an along-street shift.

- Only **two-or-more-model consensus** numerals participate. Single models read
  different subsets, at well under half mutual agreement.
- A numeral is _votable_ with a street hint, surviving era conversion, on a
  street with addressable segments. Anything else abstains individually.
- **Yes**: at least `min_numerals` in-block, a strict majority of the votable
  set, and no votable numeral beyond the `ADDR_BAND_FACTOR` abstain band.
- **No**: at least `min_numerals` votable and zero in-block.
- Anything thinner abstains.

This channel has no producer stage and costs no model spend. It reads what is
already in `annotations/`:
the escalation ladder's `p<N>.escalated.<key>.json` tier caches and the
`p<N>.v2.<key>.json` sidecars a retired stage wrote. A dedicated
consensus-annotate stage measured indistinguishable from the ladder's sidecars
and was cut; the sidecars weren't, and deleting the read path would demote
every accept standing on one. Both key shapes decode to a canonical model
identity, so one model can't self-confirm across file shapes or reasoning
efforts. Under two models' readings, it abstains.

**Eras are declared, never guessed.** A volume printed before its city's
renumbering must declare its era; numbers then convert through the city's
`RenumberingTable`. An empty table abstains, `"unknown"` abstains outright, and
`"modern"` on a pre-renumbering volume is a pass-through of numbers that aren't
today's.

### Where it sits, and the allow-list

Runs after corroborate and junction-verify. It consumes the committed voucher
pool and the _recorded_ `junction_snap` verdicts. A missing verdict abstains,
and the stage never re-runs the verifier. Non-revoked records come out
byte-identical apart from the advisory `verified_accept` audit block.

`channels` resolves from `config.evidence_channels`; `None` means every channel
may vote, which is what direct callers and golden replay get. **A channel
absent from the list does not vote even when its evidence is on disk.**
Narrowing to `["junction"]` to mute a misbehaving addresses channel has to
actually mute it, because an addresses channel reading stale evidence can veto
an otherwise good placement.

A declared channel that abstains on every page is reported as a warning. That
silence is usually a config or data fault: no consensus sidecars, missing
smalls, an absent `[cv]` extra, a model that read no numerals.

**There is no junction veto**, and its absence rests on measurement: over the
ground-truth volumes it never changed an outcome, while falsely vetoing a fifth
of correctly placed human-reviewed sheets. Dropping it can raise acceptance, so
re-measure before widening the corroboration channel further.

## Alias tables

A volume's alias table maps a historic street name read off a sheet to the key
its modern centerline is indexed under. `normalize` reads it at match time and
`centerline_key` when the index is built. **Aliases feed matching only: no
gate, funnel, or placement decision reads them.**

`alias.propose` parses the city's rename source, intersects it with the
volume's unmatched read families, keeps only values that are keys of the
volume's own bounded index, corroborates with a locality measured from sheet
evidence, then corroborates close calls with printed numerals. The tier it
assigns is the whole safety story.

| Tier              | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `clean`           | safe to write unread. A unique in-bounds candidate whose locality clears the bar on 2+ non-degenerate sheets, or an ambiguous one the locality decides by margin _and_ numerals independently support |
| `held`            | a candidate is reachable but the evidence doesn't reach the bar. A queue, not a discard pile                                                                                                          |
| `refused`         | no candidate is reachable. Kept separate from held so the queue doesn't fill with the out-of-city edge grid                                                                                           |
| `no-candidate`    | the source documents no rename for this read                                                                                                                                                          |
| `already-aliased` | a landed entry already covers the family                                                                                                                                                              |

The bars in `alias.propose` are named constants. None is an acceptance
threshold, but changing one warrants a dated record, because the clean tier
writes files.

### The rules a table must satisfy

`alias.validate` implements these once for both consumers: the sweep, which
aborts a volume rather than write a failing table, and
`scripts/validate_alias_files.py`.

- **Values are exact index keys.** The value is the lookup key, so it must
  already be the form the index files a street under: West End Ave is `END`,
  Geneva Terrace `GENEVA`, La Salle `LA SALLE`. A key the table itself creates
  on the centerline side counts.
- **No key shadows an in-bounds street.** A key that _is_ a surviving street's
  filed name takes every read of it.
- **No chains.** A value that is also a key pointing elsewhere resolves
  differently depending on lookup order. A value pointing at itself is fine and
  is how a table gives a street a key the normalizer would otherwise merge
  away.
- **No centerline joins another street's bucket.** Aliases apply to the
  centerline's own name too, so a key matching a real street's name moves that
  street's geometry into the value's bucket, and the merged shape answers for
  neither. A key whose segments all move somewhere nothing else reaches is
  renamed rather than merged, and that's allowed.

An entry that changes no outcome is exempt from all of them (`validate.is_inert`
decides by removing it and asking the normalizer). Those are dead weight rather
than defects, and the corpus instrument counts them.

**Keeping the printed direction is the shape to reach for.** `W ONTARIO`
catches only reads that print the direction, so reads of the plain name still
find the surviving street. It is not a guarantee about the centerlines:
aliases are consulted on the whole cleaned name before anything is stripped,
and hundreds of streets are named with a leading direction, so a key matching
one of their names will move it. That gets checked, not assumed.

Any entry keeping a direction or a street type takes reads away from the street
its bare form names. That's what a one-directional rename is, and also what a
mistake looks like, and nothing in the table distinguishes them, because the
question is about the city rather than the file. The validator reports these
and refuses none. Read the list; it's short.

A numbered street and the place or court beside it are two streets half a block
apart, and an alias for one is not an alias for the other. The index keeps them
separate whatever a table says, and the automated writer won't write a bare
numbered key where the twin exists in that volume.

`PRE_EXISTING` in the corpus instrument is a countdown of real violations
awaiting repair. Nothing correct belongs on it: an entry the rules shouldn't
have objected to is a rule to fix.
